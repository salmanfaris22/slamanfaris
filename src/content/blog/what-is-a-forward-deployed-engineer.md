A forward deployed engineer is a software engineer who works directly with a customer to solve that customer's real problem, often building custom software on top of a company's product. Instead of writing features for thousands of unknown users, they sit close to one team and ship what that team needs.

The role was popularised by Palantir and has become more common as companies work to get AI tools running inside real businesses.

## What the work looks like

A forward deployed engineer spends time with the people doing the work. They watch how a process runs, find where it hurts and build a fix, often in days rather than months. Then they stay close to see whether it actually helps.

That means switching often between talking and building: a call with an operations team in the morning, a data pipeline in the afternoon, and a demo the next day.

## How it differs from other roles

- **A product engineer** builds for many customers and works from a roadmap.
- **A sales engineer** shows what a product can do, and usually stops before production.
- **A forward deployed engineer** ships working software into one customer's real environment.

## Why it matters more with AI

Most AI projects fail between the demo and daily use. The model works, but it does not fit the messy data, permissions and habits of a real company. Someone has to bridge that gap, and that person needs both engineering skill and the patience to understand a business.

## Skills that help

- Full-stack development, so you can build the whole solution
- Working with messy data and old systems
- Clear communication with people who are not technical
- Good judgment about what to build and what to leave out
- Comfort with AI tools, agents and integrations

## What I have learned from this kind of work

The biggest lesson from building tools for real teams is that the first version of the problem is rarely the real one. People describe the symptom, and the cause usually shows up once you watch the work happen.
