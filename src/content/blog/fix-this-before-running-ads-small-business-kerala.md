Before you pay for ads, make sure the page they lead to can do its job. Ads only buy a visit. If that visit lands on a slow page with an unclear message and no easy way to reach you, the money is gone and you learn nothing from it.

These are the basics I check first for a small business.

## 1. Does the page load quickly on a phone?

Open your site on mobile data, or run it through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). Most visitors in Kerala arrive on their phones, and many will leave if nothing appears within a few seconds.

## 2. Is it obvious what you do?

Show the page to someone who does not know your business for five seconds, then ask what you sell and where. If they cannot answer, fix the headline first. "Quality service since 2010" says nothing. "Air conditioner repair in Kozhikode, same day" says everything.

## 3. Is the next step easy?

For many businesses here, that next step is WhatsApp. Put a clear button where people can see it, and open the chat with a message already written. Every extra step loses people.

## 4. Can you measure an enquiry?

Without tracking, you cannot tell which ad brought a customer. Set up Google Analytics and mark form submissions and WhatsApp clicks as [key events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267568). This takes an afternoon and changes every decision after it.

## 5. Does the ad match the page?

If your ad promises a course and the link goes to your home page, people have to search for what they just clicked. Send each ad to the page that matches its promise.

## 6. Do people trust you?

Real photos, real work and real reviews do more than any slogan. New visitors want proof that you exist and that others have used you. Do not invent any of it, because people notice.

## When ads make sense

Once these basics are in place, ads can be a useful way to test what people want. Until then, fixing your page usually gives a better return than a bigger budget.
