To choose a website developer in Calicut, look at their live sites on your own phone, ask who will own the code and domain, and get the scope in writing. Price matters, but those three checks tell you far more about what you will actually get.

Here is the list I would use if I were hiring someone myself.

## Open their live work on your phone

Screenshots can flatter anything. Ask for links to sites that are live right now, and open them on mobile data, because that is how most of your customers will arrive.

Check three things: how fast the page appears, whether you can use it with one thumb, and whether it is obvious how to contact the business. If their own client sites fail that test, yours probably will too.

## Ask who owns everything

Your domain, hosting account and source code should all be in your name. If a developer registers the domain for you under their own account, you can lose your website address in a disagreement. Ask before any money changes hands.

## Find out who writes the words

Many projects stall because nobody wrote the content. Agree up front whether you will supply the text and photos or the developer will. Clear words do more for enquiries than any animation.

## Ask how the site will be found

A beautiful site that nobody finds is an expensive brochure. A good developer should be able to explain page titles, a sitemap, and how Google will read the site. [Google's own starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is a useful reference for what to expect.

Ask to see the site's speed too. Google publishes [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) targets, and a developer who cares about performance will be happy to discuss them.

## Get the scope in writing

A good proposal lists the pages, the features, the number of revisions and what happens after launch. It should also say what is not included. Vague quotes often lead to surprise costs later.

## Watch how they communicate

The way someone answers your first message is a fair preview of the project. Clear questions and honest limits are good signs. Be careful of anyone who promises the first position on Google. Nobody can guarantee that.

## A quick checklist

- Live sites that work well on your phone
- Domain, hosting and code in your name
- A clear answer on who writes the content
- A plan for search, speed and tracking
- A written scope with revisions and support
- No promises of guaranteed rankings
